penlike.profile#

Profiles: what a set of texts has in common, and how far a draft is from it.

A profile is built per register, never across registers: an average over a person’s email and their technical writing describes neither. Every figure keeps the number of texts it rests on, and a profile built on little text says so (provisional).

Three operations:

  • build_profile() aggregates the measurements of many texts;

  • contrast() says what sets one profile apart from a reference;

  • compare() measures a draft against a profile and lists the discrepancies in words a writer can act on.

>>> from penlike.features import measure
>>> texts = ["Short one. Very short.", "Another short one. Tiny.", "Small again. Yes."]
>>> profile = build_profile([measure(t) for t in texts], register="notes")
>>> profile["n_docs"], profile["provisional"], profile["scalars"]["sentence_len_mean"]["n"]
(3, True, 3)

penlike.profile.build_profile(measurements, *, register='', norm=None, others=(), min_phrase_docs=3, max_phrases=40)[source]#

Aggregate the measurements of a register’s texts into its profile.

norm (from build_norm()) adds the register’s centroid and the function-word calibration: how far the register’s own texts sit from it (the ceiling for a draft) and how far texts of others registers sit (the floor).

Return type:

dict[str, Any]

penlike.profile.compare(measured, profile, *, norm=None, threshold=2.0, min_words_for_rates=30)[source]#

Measure a draft against a register’s profile and list what differs.

A discrepancy is reported when the draft sits more than threshold of the author’s own standard deviations from their average, when it shows a habit the author almost never has, or lacks one they almost always have. Each comes with the figures, so the next revision has something concrete to change.

The function-word distance, when both the draft and the profile are long enough, is reported next to two references from the author’s own texts: how far they sit from each other (own) and how far their other registers sit (others).

Return type:

dict[str, Any]

penlike.profile.contrast(profile, reference, *, top=8)[source]#

What sets profile apart from reference: the largest differences, in both directions.

Differences in averages are given in pooled standard deviations, differences in habits as shares of texts, and function words as a ratio of rates.

Return type:

dict[str, list[dict[str, Any]]]

penlike.profile.function_word_vector(measured, norm)[source]#

A text’s function-word rates as z-scores against norm, or None if too short.

This is the vector Cosine Delta compares. norm holds the mean and standard deviation of each word’s rate over a population of texts.

Return type:

list[float] | None
